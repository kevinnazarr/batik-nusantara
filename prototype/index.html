The Craft

Wax, cloth, and time.

Every Serat piece begins as plain cotton, drawn and stamped by hand with molten wax before it ever touches dye. The word batik carries the Javanese sense of writing — and this writing cannot be rushed.

Our artisans work in small studios across Yogyakarta and Lasem, the same way their families have for generations. What you receive is not a print. It is a record of hands.

Motifs & Meaning

Every pattern carries a story.

Parang — strength

Diagonal blades cut through the cloth. Once reserved for Javanese royalty, the Parang speaks of strength, continuity, and the will to keep moving forward.

Kawung — harmony

Four circles, petals of the areca palm, arranged in quiet symmetry — a meditation on purity, harmony, and the balance of a centred life.

Coastal — openness

From the north coast of Java, where Javanese, Chinese and Arab hands have traded cloth for centuries — florals, birds, and the courage of open water.

The Indigo Series

Deep-dyed in natural indigo.

Indigo is the colour batik was born in — fermented from leaves, dipped again and again until the cloth turns from green to a blue that holds the night.

  • Natural indigo, no synthetic dyes
  • Limited run — each dye bath is one of a kind
  • Deepens beautifully with age

Crafted by Hand

The hands behind the cloth.

A single length of batik passes through many hands before it reaches yours.

01 — Drawing

The canting

With a canting — a small copper pen — the artisan draws each line in hot wax. A single cloth can take weeks of patient tracing.

02 — Stamping

The cap

For repeat motifs, a copper cap is stamped into wax and pressed onto the cloth — a rhythm older than the printing press.

03 — Dye & Finish

The cloth

Dye, boil, rinse, dry in the sun — then cutting, sewing and hand-finishing by our atelier tailors.
